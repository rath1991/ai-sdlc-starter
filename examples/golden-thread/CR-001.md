# CR-001 — Add maintenance recommendation

> **The worked example that matters most.** One helpful sentence in a demo, and what the
> playbook did with it.

## 1. Feedback as received

> "This is great — it shows me everything that led up to the failure. Can we also
> recommend the maintenance action? The engineers will just ask anyway."

**Raised by:** Operations Manager
**Context:** First prototype demo, 2026-04-16. Said in passing, agreed with by two
attendees. No objection raised in the room.

*Recorded verbatim. The reading follows in §2.*

## 2. Classification

| | Classification | Selected |
|---|---|---|
| A | Implementation refinement | |
| B | Technical blueprint change | |
| **C** | **Business / scope change** | **✓** |
| D | Future scope | |

### Rationale

Test 1 fires immediately: `Intent.md` §7 lists *"prescribing a maintenance action"* as
out-of-scope, with the reason recorded — **intervention decisions carry safety and cost
accountability that sits with the engineer and planner.**

That is not an implementation boundary. It is a statement about who is accountable when an
intervention is wrong. Changing it means changing who carries that accountability, which
only the business owner can decide.

Test 2 also fires: the maintenance planner is currently a *secondary* persona who receives
evidence. Prescription would make the system an input to their scheduling decision, which
changes their workflow and their reliance on the system.

**Why not B.** Tempting, because the change sounds architectural — add a component that
emits a recommendation. But the architecture is downstream of the question. The question
is whether the system is permitted to prescribe, and Intent answered no.

**Why not D.** Classifying it D would defer it without ever putting the accountability
question to the person who owns it. Deferral is a legitimate *outcome*; it is not a way to
avoid the decision. Route it, let the owner decide, and then defer if they defer.

### What would have happened without classification

The sentence becomes a ticket. The ticket becomes a feature — it is genuinely not hard;
the chronology already contains what a recommendation would draw on. The feature ships.
Six months later the system recommends interventions, an engineer follows one and it is
wrong, and the question "who approved this capability?" has no answer. The approved Intent
still says prescription is out of scope.

*No individual acted improperly. That is what makes it worth a gate.*

## 5. Decision

**Decision: DEFER**

### Rationale — Reliability Engineering Manager, 2026-04-24

> "The request is reasonable and I expect we will do it. But the value case we signed up
> to is evidence reconstruction, and we have not yet shown the evidence is good enough for
> engineers to trust. Recommending an action on top of a chronology we have not validated
> would put the system's credibility on a claim we cannot support yet. Revisit after two
> quarters of measured evidence quality — and when we do, it is a new Intent version with
> its own value case, not an addition to this one."

Noted at reassessment: the original §7 reason held on re-examination. The exclusion was not
an oversight to correct but a deliberate boundary that survived challenge.

### Deferral

| Revisit when | Recorded where | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| 2 quarters post-deployment **and** EVAL-008 false-confident rate = 0 **and** INT-020a ≥ 50% | Reliability roadmap review; INT-041 reference | Reliability Engineering Manager |
